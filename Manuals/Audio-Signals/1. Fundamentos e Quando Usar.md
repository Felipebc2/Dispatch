# Audio-Signals: Fundamentals and When to Use It

Introductory manual for audio and simple FSK/RF signal analysis in CTFs. Walkthrough in
`2. Guia Passo a Passo.md`; competition reference in `3. Contexto para Claude.md`.

## What it is

A challenge hands you an audio file (usually a WAV) that carries data rather than music: a modem
burst, DTMF tones, SSTV image, Morse, or an SSB/FM capture. The job is to recognize the mode from
its spectrogram and run the matching decoder.

The kit:
- `sox` / `soxi`: inspect, resample, trim, split channels, and render a spectrogram.
- Audacity: visual spectrogram exploration when you are unsure of the mode.
- `minimodem`: decode (and encode) classic FSK modems, including Bell 202 and Bell 103.
- `multimon-ng`: decode AFSK1200 (AX.25/APRS), POCSAG, DTMF, and more.
- `numpy`/`scipy`: a Goertzel or FSK demodulator when the off-the-shelf tools will not lock.
- qsstv (SSTV), and simple threshold logic for Morse.

## When it is the right tool

- The attachment is `.wav`/`.flac`/`.mp3` and clearly not just a song.
- The spectrogram shows two alternating tones (FSK), tone pairs (DTMF), a swept image (SSTV), or
  on/off bursts (Morse).
- The prompt mentions radio, modem, packet, APRS, callsign, or baud.

## When it is NOT

- The flag is plain metadata or appended bytes: check `exiftool`/`binwalk`/`strings` first.
- It is audio steganography in the samples (LSB, phase): that is a stego vector, not demodulation.
- A spectrogram that spells text visually is an image task, not a decode (still use `sox`/Audacity
  to read it, but there is nothing to demodulate).
