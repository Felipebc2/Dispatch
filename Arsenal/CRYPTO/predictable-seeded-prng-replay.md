## How to recognize this pattern

- A "draw"/"lottery"/token/nonce is generated by a language PRNG **seeded by the current time**:
  `new Random((int)UnixTimeSeconds())` (.NET), `random.seed(int(time.time()))` (Python),
  `srand(time(NULL))` (C), `new Random(System.currentTimeMillis())` (Java).
- You win on a specific output (`draw == 42`), and the client/binary is handed to you. The seed space
  is tiny (seconds in a window) and the generator is **deterministic and portable**.

## Method

### 1. Recover the exact generator and seed source
Reverse the draw: which PRNG, seeded by what, reduced how. E.g. `.NET`'s **seeded** `Random(int)` is
the legacy Knuth subtractive PRNG — identical on every platform — and `(Random(s).Next() % 1000)+1`
with `s = unix_second`.

### 2. Reproduce it offline and port the generator
Port the PRNG and **self-test against a canonical anchor** (for .NET: `new Random(0).Next() ==
1559595546`). Then scan the seed range (the test window ± skew) for seeds that produce the winning
value:
```python
for s in range(start_unix, end_unix):
    if draw(s) == 42:
        print(s, datetime.utcfromtimestamp(s))
```

### 3. Exploit the seed timeline
If the engine **reseeds to "now" after each draw**, the *next* draw is locked by the second at which
you clicked the *previous* one — both attacker-controlled to ~1 s. Click a throwaway draw exactly at a
precomputed winning second `W` (sets seed = W), then draw again → `f(W)` = win. Harden for clock skew
by firing the setup draws on `W-1, W, W+1`.

## Gotchas

- **Seeded `Random(int)` ≠ the thread-safe/crypto RNG.** A fixed int seed is the legacy deterministic
  generator; that is exactly what makes it reproducible. (Python `random` = Mersenne Twister, also fully
  predictable from a known seed or 624 consecutive outputs.)
- No decompiler needed if installs are blocked — a hand-rolled IL/bytecode dump plus the reproduced
  generator is enough (see `dotnet-il-static-analysis`).
- The winning secret (flag) is often **per-team, server-side**, returned only on a real win — so you
  must actually hit the output in the live window, not just read it from the binary.
- Sync to the **server/test machine's** clock (the seed uses *its* `UtcNow`); assume NTP, let a ±1-2 s
  tolerance absorb skew.
